Why clarity matters

Small changes can create big impacts.

Do you know the cost of a 15% fuel increase?

Fuel changes affect transport, logistics, delivery schedules, and margins. A 15% increase can shift profitability across multiple jobs or customers. Clear reporting shows the real impact before it becomes a financial surprise.

Have you planned for future shocks?

Supplier delays, cost spikes, overtime surges, and demand changes can hit without warning. Understanding your current position makes it easier to prepare, adjust, and respond quickly.

Are hidden costs affecting performance?

Missed follow-ups, slow purchasing cycles, rising labour hours, and small inefficiencies add up fast. Clear reporting makes these issues visible so they can be addressed early.

Case study

How clearer reporting changed a business.

Background

A mid-sized manufacturing and distribution business was struggling with rising costs, late supplier deliveries, and overtime that seemed to grow every quarter. Information was scattered across spreadsheets, emails, and accounting systems, making it difficult for managers to understand what was actually happening.

Problems identified

After reviewing their data and processes, several issues became clear:

  • Purchase orders were raised inconsistently — sometimes per item, sometimes for entire jobs.
  • Invoices were rarely checked against deliveries, leading to unnoticed overcharges.
  • Overtime was increasing, but no one could explain why or link it to output.
  • Supplier delays were recorded informally, making it hard to track reliability.
  • Managers were making decisions without a clear picture of costs or timelines.

What was delivered

Using the data they already had, I built:

  • A sales and purchasing dashboard showing orders, delays, cost changes, and supplier performance.
  • A labour and overtime report linking hours worked to production output.
  • A simple purchasing workflow that clarified how and when to raise POs.
  • A basic invoice-checking process to ensure charges matched deliveries.
  • Training for staff on record-keeping, purchasing discipline, and basic cost awareness.

Results

Within three months, the business saw measurable improvements:

  • Overtime reduced by 18% after identifying unnecessary hours and staffing imbalances.
  • Supplier delays dropped once performance was visible and discussed with vendors.
  • Invoice discrepancies were caught early, preventing repeat overcharging.
  • Managers gained confidence in planning because they finally had reliable information.
  • Staff understood their processes better and felt more capable in their roles.
